# Material and Finish Fidelity

Read this reference whenever material appearance affects product identity, perceived quality, or user requirements. Match the user's physical reference, not a generic “premium” treatment.

## Material specification

Define five fields before rendering or editing:

1. Base material.
2. Surface process or coating.
3. Gloss/roughness level.
4. Direction and scale of texture.
5. Lighting/reference image used to judge the finish.

If any field is unknown and alternative answers would visibly change the product, use the material reminder in [product-intake.md](product-intake.md) before producing the product master.

## Common finish cues

### Brushed stainless steel

- Show fine, coherent linear micro-scratches following the manufactured brushing direction.
- Use elongated anisotropic highlights and restrained reflections.
- Retain neutral-to-cool steel color and readable edge highlights.
- Reject mirror-like chrome, random cross-hatching, coarse scratches, glitter, or flat gray plastic appearance.

### Polished stainless steel or chrome plating

- Use crisp environmental reflections and high specular contrast while preserving the product silhouette.
- Reflections must follow curvature and panel orientation.
- Reject uniformly white metal, muddy gray surfaces, or reflections that look painted onto the object.

### Aluminum and anodized aluminum

- Keep highlights softer and the apparent density lighter than polished steel.
- Anodized color should retain fine metallic response beneath the tint.
- Reject plastic-looking color, heavy chrome reflections, or an orange-peel coating unless the reference shows it.

### Powder-coated or painted metal

- Use diffuse matte or satin highlights with subtle coating texture.
- Preserve coated edges without exposing raw metal unless wear is requested.
- For black finishes, avoid clipping the object to featureless pure black; preserve rim light and local form.

### Brass, gold-tone, and PVD finishes

- Confirm whether the product is solid brass, plated, painted, or PVD-coated; these are not interchangeable claims.
- Match warm/cool hue, reflectivity, and brushing direction to the reference.
- Reject yellow plastic appearance, excessive orange cast, and inconsistent gold across pages.

### Plastic

- Confirm resin appearance when known: glossy ABS, matte ABS, translucent polycarbonate, acrylic, or soft-touch coating.
- Preserve realistic wall thickness, molded radii, seams, gates, and texture only where visible in the source.
- Reject metal-like highlights on ordinary plastic or arbitrary molding seams.

### Transparent glass or acrylic

- Preserve thickness through edge highlights, refraction, internal reflections, and partial background visibility.
- Keep transparent parts visible without turning them opaque or making them disappear.
- Distinguish glass from acrylic only when evidence supports it.

### Silicone and rubber

- Use soft, broad highlights, fine matte microtexture, and plausible deformation only at real contact points.
- Reject hard plastic gloss or unexplained dents.

### Wood

- Match species/color only when supported. Grain follows the manufactured part and remains continuous across a surface.
- Preserve pores, end grain, joints, and finish level at the scale visible in the reference.
- Reject tiled grain, grain crossing separate parts unrealistically, or synthetic-looking contrast.

### Textile and leather

- Match weave or grain scale, nap, seams, piping, stitching, folds, and tension to the product construction.
- Keep patterns aligned to panels and perspective.
- Reject melted texture, inconsistent weave scale, disappearing seams, or leather grain applied like a flat overlay.

### Ceramic and glazed surfaces

- Use smooth dielectric highlights, believable glaze depth, and subtle edge variation.
- Preserve handmade irregularity only when shown by the real product.
- Reject plastic translucency or metallic reflections.

## Lighting for material proof

- Use broad controlled highlights to reveal curvature and surface continuity.
- Add a close-up detail frame when the finish is a selling point.
- Keep lighting consistent enough across variants for fair comparison.
- Separate the object from the background without recoloring the finish.
- Treat a style reference as a lighting/composition reference unless the user explicitly authorizes transferring its material.

## Material QA

A material passes only when the base material, process, gloss, texture direction, scale, hue, and reflection behavior match the confirmed brief across every visible instance. Report any part whose material cannot be verified from the supplied evidence.
