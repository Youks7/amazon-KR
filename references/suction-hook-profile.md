# Historical Example: Suction Hook Product Profile

This is an optional worked example, not the scope of the Skill. Use it only for a product that matches the described suction-hook geometry. Current user-supplied product sources outrank this historical profile whenever they conflict.

## Geometry invariants

- One circular suction cup with one circular front knob.
- A short neck connects the suction assembly to a normal-width horizontal bracket.
- The bracket has exactly two U-shaped razor slots.
- The two outer ends turn upward and can carry rings or cord loops.
- In the historical front-facing master, keep the suction cup circular; reject non-uniform scaling. In an oblique view, a circular face may project as an ellipse. Judge its projection against the source angle rather than forcing it into a circle.
- Do not lengthen the bracket, add slots, merge the outer hooks into the slots, or redraw the silhouette between pages.

## Approved use/contact mapping

| Finish | Approved demonstration | Required contact logic |
|---|---|---|
| Warm brushed gold | Two razors | One handle in each original U-slot; wider heads stop above the openings |
| Soft satin black | Keys and handled brush | Key ring over the left outer upturned hook; brush cord loop over the right outer upturned hook |
| Cool brushed silver | Razor and shower pouf | Razor handle in the left slot; pouf cord loop over the right outer upturned hook |

Objects fail review if they float, intersect metal, adhere to the flat bracket face, or merely sit near the product.

## Installation sequence represented in the approved set

1. Clean and dry the target surface.
2. Loosen the knob.
3. Press the suction cup flat.
4. Turn the knob to lock.
5. Release, rinse, and reuse.

Reuse one fixed black product asset across the mechanical steps. Use deterministic arrows and symbols rather than AI-generated hands when hand/product interaction would obscure or mutate the mechanism.

## Surface guide represented in the approved set

- Shown as suitable: glass, mirror, glazed tile, smooth metal.
- Shown as unsuitable: textured tile, raw wood, wallpaper.
- The shared principle in the approved copy is “smooth, flat, non-porous.” Treat this as the project's represented guidance; do not broaden it without product evidence.

## Project output families

- The historical final A+ set contained six JPEG files at exactly 970 × 300 px.
- Historical derivatives also used 600 × 450 and 1464 × 600. These are evidence of project-specific requested formats, not proof of current marketplace-wide requirements.

The historical V3 workflow used approved fixed assets in preference to earlier generated masters. Earlier drafts contained documented geometry drift, inconsistent finishes, false transparency, edge stretching, clipped titles, and invalid contact points.
